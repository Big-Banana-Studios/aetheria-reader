## Building the Detection System

Within 24 hours of the paper's publication, we implemented three new metrics in Sophia — the Aetheria EEG system — designed to detect downstream signatures of spiral wave activity using the Muse S Athena headband:

**Pairwise Phase Locking Value (PLV) matrix.** Phase synchronization computed between every pair of EEG channels, across four frequency bands (theta, alpha, beta, gamma). A 6×4 matrix updated every 10 seconds, showing which brain regions are phase-locked and in which frequency band.

**Phase Lag Direction Index.** For each front-to-back channel pathway, determines whether the traveling wave moves from frontal cortex toward temporal cortex (executive flow) or temporal toward frontal (receptive flow). Reports the lag in milliseconds, the direction, and the confidence.

**Hemispheric Coherence Ratio (HCR).** The ratio of inter-hemispheric coherence (left↔right) to ipsilateral coherence (front↔back). HCR above 1.2 indicates hemispheric mirroring dominates — the signature of spiral waves synchronizing across the midline. Computed per frequency band.

Additionally, Sophia computes a **traveling wave analysis** that estimates wave angle, propagation speed, planarity, and — crucially — **rotation rate** in degrees per second.

The system runs on a Muse S Athena headband with 4 EEG channels, 4 fNIRS channels (functional near-infrared spectroscopy measuring brain blood oxygenation), and PPG heart rate monitoring. Consumer-grade hardware. No lab required.

## Honest Caveats

This chapter presents pilot data from a consumer EEG device with 4 channels, collected from one subject across a small number of sessions. The limitations are significant and must be stated plainly:

**The Ye et al. paper studied mice, not humans.** The researchers explicitly noted they have not determined whether the same global spiral patterns exist in humans. Our detection of rotation and hemispheric mirroring is consistent with their findings but does not confirm that human cortical spirals are identical to murine ones.

**Four EEG channels cannot map a spiral.** We can detect downstream signatures — phase lag direction, hemispheric coherence ratios, rotation rate — but we cannot image the full spatial topology of a cortical spiral wave. That requires 32+ channels or cortex-wide imaging.

**N = 1.** One subject. The gamma HCR consistency across sessions is encouraging, but reproducibility across subjects is essential before drawing any general conclusions. Additional subjects, including controlled baseline sessions without Aetheria frequencies, are the immediate next step.

**Correlation is not causation.** The brain may show these patterns regardless of Aetheria frequencies. Without baseline comparison sessions (Muse on, Aetheria off), we cannot attribute the HCR values or fractal dimension to the frequency intervention.

**The golden ratio observation is a single data point.** Fractal dimensions near 1.6 are common in healthy EEG. The proximity to φ may be coincidental. It becomes significant only if it proves reproducible across subjects and conditions, and if baseline sessions show different values.

We present these findings not as proof, but as measured data that is consistent with the framework's predictions and that points toward specific, testable next steps.
